# Reference images: sources and licences

Ten reference images for the Artesian Water film, chosen for true-to-life machinery, strikes and bore drains. All were downloaded on 2026-09-24. Full research notes are in `../RESEARCH.md`.

Licence notes: "PD" means public domain. Wikimedia Commons licence statements are copied from each file page. For the book scans, the licence is inferred from the publication date. Anything published before 1931 is public domain in the US. Where the author died more than 70 years ago it is public domain in most other countries as well.

| `01_cox1895_canadian_pole_drill_rig_diagram.jpg` | Page 109 of Cox 1895, "Artesian Boring Plant, Canadian Pole Drill". This is the Queensland textbook diagram of the whole rig. It shows the derrick (dimension line reads 47 ft) with its ladder and two stagings (I, J) and the crown pulleys (G, O). It also shows the hoisting rope, the "pole of drill" going down the bore, the rocking (walking) beam B on its pivot D, the crank pulley A with connecting rod C, the drums E and K, the belts, and a portable engine with a tall stack. The text below the diagram explains the lettering. | https://archive.org/details/cu31924004406827 (scan leaf n144; image https://archive.org/download/cu31924004406827/page/n144.jpg) | Walter Gibbons Cox, *Artesian Wells as a Means of Water Supply*, Brisbane: Sapsford & Co., 1895. | `02_thompson1910_canadian_rig_spring_pole_slipper_out.jpg` | Fig. 51, "Canadian Rig", with the string of tools drawn on the left: drill poles, jars, sinker, bit. This is the clearest drawing of how the pole-tool string hangs from the beam. | https://archive.org/details/petroleumminingo00thomuoft (leaf n262; image https://archive.org/download/petroleumminingo00thomuoft/page/n262.jpg) | A. | `03_cox1895_drilling_at_marathon_station_qld.jpg` | Halftone photo, "Drilling at Marathon Station, Queensland". The walking beam and frame run out to a portable steam engine with a big flywheel and a tall stack. ## Further candidates, not downloaded

The coordinator capped this set at about ten files. These are all public domain on Commons unless noted, and each is worth fetching if a shot needs it:

- Tinnenburra No. 4 bore, 1891. Water rises over the casing as a smooth "glass dome", the shape described at Barcaldine in 1887. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_178059_Water_bore_Tinnenburra_No._4_at_Tinnenburra_Station,_1891.jpg
- Bundaleer No. 6, 1890. A tall fountain of water over the casing. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_394721_Water_bore_on_Bundaleer_Plains_Station,_Queensland,_1890.jpg
- Charlotte Plains No. 1, 1891. A flowing jet beside a timber sheer-leg frame, with a ladder, spoil heaps and a wagon wheel. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_295295_Water_bore_at_Charlotte_Plains_station,_1891.jpg
- Leichhardt Station, 1895. A timber derrick and a horse team. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_156767_Sinking_an_artesian_bore_on_Leichhardt_Station,_Queensland,_1895.jpg
- J. S. Henderson's well-boring plant, Ilfracombe, 1898 (a newspaper halftone). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_1_47752_J._S._Henderson%27s_well_boring_plant_in_the_Ilfracombe_area,_1898.jpg
- Winton bore, 1895. The Premier and visitors under the rig timbers, showing the beam and the drum. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SirHughNelsonWintonQueenslandBore1895.png (from Corfield, *Reminiscences of Queensland*, 1921)
- Houston's Artesian Well Boring Plant, 1900. A walking beam and drums on a horse-drawn wagon. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_127953_Artesian_bore_drilling_plant_in_Queensland,_1900.jpg
- Saltern Creek No. 2 bore, 1899. A man beside a pipe spout, on treeless downs. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saltern_Creek_bore_No_2,_1899.jpg
- Charlotte Plains bore, 1906. A tall column of water (Kerry & Co.). https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Irrigation_with_surface_and_subterranean_waters_and_land_drainage_with_special_reference_to_the_geological_development_and_utilisation_of_artesian_and_sub-artesian_supplies_(Page_37)_BHL42821427.jpg
- Sub-artesian well borers near Cloncurry, 1908. A small rig with a vertical-boiler engine; two workmen in work clothes and a dog. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:StateLibQld_2_153391_Sub_artesian_well_borers_near_Cloncurry,_Queensland,_1908.jpg
- Camp at Bore No. 4 Tinnenburra, c. 1910. A canvas fly against a buggy, with a meat carcass hung up, saddles and a rifle. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Queensland_State_Archives_3208_Camp_at_Bore_No_4_Tinnenburra_c_1910.png
- Native Dog bore, NSW, from Cox 1895. A tall column with a side outlet (1,700,000 gal a day, 92 F). https://archive.org/details/cu31924004406827 (leaf n128)
- Charleville and Muckadilla bore strata sections, from Cox 1895. Useful for the "dive" cross-section. https://archive.org/details/cu31924004406827 (leaf n109)
- Isler, *Well-Boring for Water, Brine and Oil*: tool plates showing the chisel, jar bar, bailer, temper screw, sand pump, rod wrench and chisel gauge, plus Fig. 76 "Canadian Boring Gear". https://archive.org/details/wellboringforwat00isle (leaves n186-n191). This is a 1921 edition, so it is PD in the US only.
