# Artesian Water: storyboard

**Logline.** A drought is killing the station's stock, so a bore crew stops praying and drills: through clay, through the devil's own roof, through bent rods, jammed tubes and a broke contractor, until at four thousand feet the rock gives and the water comes up.

**Look.** Coloured pencil and graphite on toned (sand) paper, a 1940s working-man's illustration (Saturday Evening Post derrick men, Benton's muscle and lean). Short hatching strokes that boil 12x a second, sketchy doubled contours, pale-pencil highlights on the toned ground. No watercolour. The ballad is hand-lettered in graphite along a torn strip at the foot of the page, written on as it's sung, **on screen the whole time**.

**Hero.** The driller (`CAST.driller`): huge, black-bearded, singlet and braces, slouch hat. John Henry: every beat of the chorus is a blow he lands. Around him: Canadian Bill the fireman (`CAST.bill`), the boss (`CAST.boss`), two hands (`CAST.hand1`, `CAST.hand2`), the squatter (`CAST.squatter`), the canny Scot (`CAST.scot`, Glasgow only) and the devil (chapter B, reused in G).

**Motif.** *The dive.* Every chorus ends by falling down the bore through a cross-section of the earth to the bit, which strikes on the beat (`dive()`), and every dive reaches deeper, past the devil's hall, the coal and the fossils, to the granite and finally the blue water-sand. After the strike (chorus 7) the dive runs **up**: water racing to the surface. The depth ruler with its rust marker is the running score.

**Colour arc.** Drought ochre and white sun (A) → dusty afternoon and hell-red below (B) → golden late afternoon, grey Glasgow flashback (C) → teal night under lanterns, the reference painting (D) → blistering white-hot noon (E) → storm-dark dusk, red effort (F) → dawn gold and the first blue (G) → cerulean water and sap green (H).

**Rhyme.** Opening: a cattle skull on cracked mud by a dry waterhole, a still windmill, the white sun. Ending: the same frame, the waterhole full and glinting, grass through the skull, the windmill turning, sheep drinking, the derrick small on the horizon, then the pencil lifts off to blank paper under the title.

**Tempo.** 112.35 BPM (beat 0.534 s, bar 2.136 s), first beat 0.24 s. Verse lines ~4.3 s (two bars). Chorus lines ~2.9 s.

## Chapters

Times are video seconds (lyric line numbers from `src/lyrics.js` in brackets).

### A · 0.00–47.90 · Drought (intro, verse 1 [0–4], chorus 1 [5–8], instrumental)
- Intro 0–7.4: blank paper; the opening frame sketches itself in (contours draw on, then colour hatches in): skull on cracked mud, dry waterhole, still windmill, white sun. Title lettered in the band. Heat shimmer, a crow lands on the skull.
- [0] stock dying: a thin sheep staggers, folds down in the heat; crows wait on a dead gidgee.
- [1] prayers: the squatter on his homestead veranda looks up at an empty sky, hands clasped; nothing; he jams his hat on and strides off toward the crew. Acted change: hope → disgust → resolve.
- [2] derricks above, earth below: camera tilts up the timber derrick against the sky, then down its legs to the bore hole in the ground.
- [3] waiting at the lever: close on the driller's hand on the brake lever, the boss with his watch, crew poised, Bill at the firebox. Held tension on the beat.
- [4] let her go: the boss drops his arm, the lever is thrown, the flywheel lurches, the walking beam drops, the first blow kicks up dust.
- Chorus 1: crew at the rig in rhythm, then `dive()` 0 → ~600 ft.
- Instrumental 41.5–47.9: days pass: shadows sweep, the sun arcs, rods added one after another, the bit going down.

### B · 47.90–81.24 · The devil's roof (verse 2 [9–13], chorus 2 [14–17])
- [9] a thousand feet: dive-style section, bit plugging at ~1000 ft on the ruler.
- [10] if the Lord won't…: the boss/driller shakes a fist at the sky, then grins and points DOWN.
- [11] knocking on the roof of Satan's dwelling: section: under the bit, a red-lit cavern: the devil in an armchair with a teacup; each knock (on the beat) shakes dust on him; he looks up, annoyed.
- [12] cave the roof of hell in: the bit breaks through; a lump of ceiling drops; he dodges, furious, tea everywhere.
- [13] from the devil: the devil shakes his fist up the shaft; above, the crew laugh.
- Chorus 2: crew rhythm; dive passes the devil's hall (he glares as the bit goes by) to ~1300 ft.

### C · 81.24–116.30 · The Glasgow engine (verse 3 [18–22], chorus 3 [23–26])
- [18] built in Glasgow: grey rainy foundry vignette (desaturated, drawn like an old engraving): the canny Scot taps the boiler, pleased.
- [19] twenty horse-power: he stamps a brass plate "20 H.P." (the one allowed lettering here), winks.
- [20] Canadian Bill firing: back to the outback, golden light: Bill hurls gidgee logs into the firebox, fire roars on his face.
- [21] thirty horses and a score of dogs: the smoke from the stack becomes galloping horses and running dogs drawn in the smoke; the flywheel blurs.
- [22] bound to get the water: engine at full chuff, beam pounding, crew cheer.
- Chorus 3: rhythm under a setting sun; dive through shale into coal and past a fossil skeleton to ~2100 ft.

### D · 116.30–151.94 · Night trouble (verse 4 [27–31], chorus 4 [32–35])
- [27] shaft caving: section: the shaft walls crumble, sand pours in round the bit.
- [28] yellow rods bending: the pine rods bow in muddy water down below.
- [29] tubes jamming: THE REFERENCE PAINTING: teal night, lanterns, three men wrestling the pipe with tongs on the derrick floor, derricks behind. Nothing moves.
- [30] forty horse-power lift: the engine shakes, gauge needle into the red, steam jets, Bill stokes; the pipe suddenly frees and the men stagger.
- [31] stubborn drill ramming: the beam slams down, the bit rams.
- Chorus 4: night rhythm by lantern; dive in dark rock to ~2700 ft.

### E · 151.94–189.20 · Ruin (verse 5 [36–40], chorus 5 [41–44])
- [36] ruin to the squatter: the squatter at his veranda table, a bank letter, the last thin mob of sheep behind him.
- [37] weather growing hotter: the sun swells white; a thermometer on the veranda post bursts its top.
- [38] passed three thousand feet: the bailer comes up the hole: empty, dust pours out. Ruler at 3000.
- [39] boss nearly beat: the boss slumps on a crate counting coins, the contract paper, sweat.
- [40] bound to get the water: the driller puts a hand on the boss's shoulder, picks up the sledge. Resolve.
- Chorus 5: JOHN HENRY: the driller alone, swinging the sledge on the beat on the drill collar, sparks, the crew rallying behind him; dive to ~3500 ft.

### F · 189.20–225.55 · Bedrock (verse 6 [45–49], chorus 6 [50–53], the hush)
- [45] must be beneath us: the driller kneels, ear to the casing, listening; the section below shows (dramatic irony) the faint blue of water under the granite.
- [46] solid rock, four thousand feet: the bit bounces off granite, sparks, the ruler at 4000.
- [47] hammer, splice and bully: montage on the beat: a rod snaps; the driller hammers it straight on an anvil; they splice it.
- [48] fencing-wire: twisting fencing wire round the broken beam and the engine; it holds.
- [49] wring it from the bedrock: the whole crew on the tongs, the driller's arms knotted, turning the string by main force.
- Chorus 6: storm-dark dusk, max effort, everyone swinging on the beat; the dive reaches 4040 ft and a hairline crack of blue opens in the granite on the last blow.
- 223.3–225.55 the hush: everything stops; the crew freeze, listening; a rumble; the ground trembles; dust rises off the derrick floor.

### G · 225.55–257.68 · The strike, FINALE (verse 7 [54–58], chorus 7 [59–62])
- [54] whistle blowing: burst of white steam: Bill yanks the whistle cord, the engine screams steam into a dawn sky.
- [55] boys madly cheering: hats thrown high, men embracing, the boss dancing a jig, the driller roaring.
- [56] rushing up the tubing: reversed dive: the camera races UP the shaft with the water, past the granite, the fossil, the coal, the devil (doused, steam off him), the clay.
- [57] spouts above the casing: the geyser erupts over the derrick, a million-gallon column against a gold sunrise, a rainbow in the spray.
- [58] flowing, ever flowing: the crew under the falling water, soaked, the driller arms wide.
- Chorus 7: celebration in the spray, on the beat; the squatter arrives running; sheep come running to the water.

### H · 257.68–292.80 · The land again (verse 8 [63–67], final chorus [68–73])
- [63] clear away the timber: the crew clear logs and cut a channel, the water runs down the bore drain.
- [64] glimmers in the shadow, flashes in the sun: close on water running under trees, glints.
- [65] belts of timber, miles of blazing plain: tracking along the stream across the plain, trees and plain passing.
- [66] hope and comfort to the thirsty land: stock drinking, green shoots, the squatter kneeling with water in his hands.
- [67] flowing further down: the stream winds away toward the horizon.
- Final chorus (3 pairs): the crew silhouetted at the rig, a last blow for luck; then the rhyme of the opening frame, green and full; pull back; the pencil lifts off the page to blank paper; the band holds the last line.
